Bio

I am a structural engineer and researcher. I studied civil engineering at the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú and practiced as a structural engineer for five years before moving to the Netherlands, where I completed an MSc in Structural Engineering at Delft University of Technology (cum laude).

I now work as a researcher at TNO in the Netherlands, and I am pursuing a PhD at ETH Zürich, sponsored by TNO, on the structural safety of populations of similar buildings.

Research

PhD project: Population-based structural reliability

My PhD develops methods for assessing the structural safety of buildings, not one building at a time, but across whole populations of them. Conventional safety checks treat each building as a unique, isolated object, even though buildings that share a structural type, materials, and loading environment also tend to share how they behave and how they fail.

My research treats such similar buildings as a group, so that the sparse information available for any single one of them (its age and survival history, an inspection, monitoring data, a load test) can be pooled and transferred across the population to sharpen the reliability estimate for all of them. The work combines mechanical (finite element) modelling with Bayesian inference, and uses timber buildings as its main case studies.

The PhD is supervised by Eleni Chatzi at ETH Zürich and Raphaël Steenbergen at TNO.

Past research

My earlier work covers system-level building reliability (assessing how entire structural systems, rather than single components, reach failure), probabilistic assessment of timber connections and glulam beams from laboratory data (finding that Eurocode resistance estimates are substantially conservative), reliability updating with amortized Bayesian inference, vibration-based monitoring of high-rise buildings within the HiViBe consortium, and reliability updating from operational evidence such as survival histories and proof load tests.
